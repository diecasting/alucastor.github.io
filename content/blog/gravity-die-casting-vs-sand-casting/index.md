Two of the most common questions we get at Alucastor start the same way: *"Should this be gravity die cast or sand cast?"* The honest answer is that it depends on four things — volume, size, mechanical requirements and budget — and the right choice can swing the total cost of a part by a factor of three. Here is how we reason through it.

## The Core Difference

Gravity die casting pours molten aluminum into a **reusable steel mold** (usually H13 tool steel). The mold is expensive to make but lasts for tens of thousands of shots, and its metal walls chill the casting quickly.

Aluminum sand casting forms each part in a **single-use mold packed from sand** around a reusable pattern. The pattern is cheap and fast to make, but a fresh mold is destroyed for every casting, and sand insulates rather than chills.

Everything else — cost curve, finish, strength, geometry freedom — follows from that one distinction.

## Tooling Cost and Volume Break-Even

- **Sand casting** wins on low volume. A pattern is a fraction of the cost of a permanent mold and can be ready in 7–12 days. For 1–500 pieces a year, spare parts, or a prototype that must behave like the production casting, sand is almost always the lower total cost.
- **Gravity die casting** wins on volume. The H13 mold takes 25–35 days and a meaningful investment, but the per-piece cost is low and stable. Above roughly a few hundred to a couple of thousand pieces per year, the tooling amortises and gravity casting pulls ahead.

## Surface Finish and Wall Thickness

The steel mold of gravity casting gives a smoother as-cast surface and holds thinner, more repeatable walls. Sand casting carries the grain of the mold medium, so it generally needs more machining or finishing and is better suited to heavier sections. If your part has thin walls, tight cosmetic requirements, or many machined faces, gravity casting usually saves money downstream even when the tooling costs more.

## Mechanical Properties

Because the metal mold chills the aluminum faster, gravity die casting produces a finer grain and higher, more consistent mechanical properties for the same alloy and T6 treatment. Sand casting's slower cooling can actually help on very large, thick castings where a die would cause stress, but for most structural parts under 40 kg, gravity casting gives the better property profile.

## Geometry and Internal Passages

Sand casting is the more flexible process for complex internal passages and undercuts, because sand cores can be placed and then broken out afterward. Permanent molds are limited by the need to open and eject the part. For a manifold full of internal channels, sand — or 3D printed sand — is often the only practical casting route.

## Quick Decision Guide

| Factor | Choose Gravity Die Casting | Choose Sand Casting |
|---|---|---|
| Annual volume | Medium to high | Low, or one-off |
| Part weight | 0.1–40 kg | Large / heavy sections |
| Surface finish | Smooth, cosmetic | Machined or non-critical |
| Internal passages | Simple | Complex, cored |
| Tooling budget | Higher, amortised | Minimal |
| Lead time to first part | 25–35 days | 7–12 days |
